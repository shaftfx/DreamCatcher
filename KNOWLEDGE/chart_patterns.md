# Chart Patterns — quick reference

Distilled from the user's *Technical Analysis Mastery* course, Section 3.

Chart patterns play out over weeks, not minutes. They fit the swing horizon. Use these to identify setups; combine with fundamental thesis from `STRATEGY.md`.

Each pattern below has: **shape**, **direction**, **trigger**, **target**, **invalidation**.

---

## Continuation patterns

### Bull Flag
- **Shape**: Sharp rally (the "pole"), then a tight orderly pullback that drifts down or sideways (the "flag").
- **Direction**: bullish continuation.
- **Trigger**: close above flag's upper trend line on volume.
- **Target**: pole height projected from breakout point.
- **Invalidation**: close below flag's lower trend line.

### Bear Flag
- Mirror of bull flag, in a downtrend. Bull doesn't trade these (we're long-only).

### Ascending Triangle
- **Shape**: flat resistance ceiling, rising trend line of higher lows.
- **Direction**: bullish (most often breaks up).
- **Trigger**: close above the flat ceiling on volume.
- **Target**: triangle height projected up from breakout.
- **Invalidation**: close below the rising trend line.

### Descending Triangle
- Mirror: flat floor, lower highs. Bearish. Bull doesn't enter; if we hold one breaking down, exit per stops.

### Symmetrical Triangle
- **Shape**: lower highs and higher lows converging to an apex.
- **Direction**: ambiguous — breaks in the direction of the prior trend ~70% of the time.
- **Trigger**: close outside one of the trend lines on volume.
- Bull only enters on **upside breaks** in an uptrend context.

### Cup and Handle
- **Shape**: rounded "U" recovery (cup), then a small pullback (handle).
- **Direction**: bullish continuation.
- **Trigger**: close above the cup's right-side high (the "rim") on rising volume.
- **Target**: cup depth projected up from breakout.
- **Invalidation**: handle pullback exceeds 50% retracement of the cup.

## Reversal patterns

### Double Bottom
- **Shape**: two distinct lows at roughly the same level, separated by a peak ("the neckline" connects highs).
- **Direction**: bullish reversal.
- **Trigger**: close above the neckline.
- **Target**: depth from the lows to the neckline, projected up.
- **Invalidation**: close below the second low.

### Double Top
- Mirror: bearish reversal. Bull exits longs that form double tops.

### Inverse Head & Shoulders
- **Shape**: three lows — middle one (the "head") deeper than the two "shoulders." Neckline connects the highs.
- **Direction**: bullish reversal.
- **Trigger**: close above the neckline.
- **Target**: head-to-neckline distance projected up.
- **Invalidation**: close below the right shoulder's low.

### Head & Shoulders
- Mirror: bearish reversal. Bull exits longs.

### Falling Wedge
- **Shape**: two converging downward-sloping trend lines; lower line falls faster than upper.
- **Direction**: bullish reversal.
- **Trigger**: close above upper trend line on volume.
- **Target**: widest part of the wedge projected up.
- **Invalidation**: close below the lower trend line.

### Rising Wedge
- Mirror: bearish reversal. Bull exits longs.

## How Bull uses chart patterns

1. **Pattern detection is judgment, not pixel-perfect.** Use the daily chart of the last 60–120 bars. If you can describe the shape in words and point to clear trend lines, it counts.
2. **Volume confirms.** A breakout without volume expansion is suspect. Look for the breakout day's volume to be ≥ 1.5× the 20-day average.
3. **Combine with the entry framework in `STRATEGY.md`.** A pattern alone is not enough — there must be a fundamental thesis. The pattern is timing.
4. **Stops go below the pattern.** For longs: stop at the pattern's invalidation point (e.g. below the flag, below the right shoulder).

Patterns are heuristics — not certainties. Treat each setup as a probabilistic edge, sized per `RULES.md`.
